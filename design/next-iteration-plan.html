How work continues

Use the agreed designer, builder, independent critic and verifier roles. Give each a broad objective and room for judgment. The builder owns game edits; build and emulator control pass explicitly between agents. The character reviewer checks original photographs whenever Kittie changes.

Each whole level gets one independent full review, followed by targeted fixes and rechecks. Continue to the next planned item when the current item meets its completion criteria. Avoid starting another broad critique merely to seek more polish. Keep a persistent record of the current build, findings, resolved issues and next action so work can resume after an interruption.

Present each completed level as a reviewable build with a short description and a genuine gameplay capture. Human review is needed for a substantial new mechanic, a character redesign, a change to the established difficulty or resource rules, or release publication. Routine tuning, source repairs, native verification and fixes to agreed behavior stay within the team. A review update need not pause subsequent work within an agreed plan.

Keep the scope stable

  • Use the approved four-stage order. The banquet and photoshoot remain the ending. Further levels are outside this iteration.
  • Keep balls physical and scarce. Their two uses remain bells and enemies. Preserve whole-stage retry and constant-speed goons.
  • Keep the scoring rule: 100 per defeated goon and 200 per ball retained at completion, with a separate best for each stage.
  • Boss or rival work remains on hold. Shops, currency and upgrade systems are outside this iteration.
  • Choose a few environmental improvements per level. Do not add every suggested effect.
