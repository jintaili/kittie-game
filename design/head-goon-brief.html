Kittie Has Other Plans · Implementation brief · 3 October 2026

Head Goon at the service passage

Historical first design. The owner rejected its stationary winning strategy. Use the approved aimed-hop revision for current development.

Build a short, separate encounter after Villa and before Banquet. A larger, cute cloth goon carries a serving tray as a shield. Kittie reads the tray, finds an opening, throws, and recovers the same physical wool ball. Three deliberate hits open the passage. The recommended layout is three screens with one fixed-camera fighting area, one ball and no lethal terrain.

The dependable solution is a ground-level throw when the tray lowers at a patrol endpoint. The lift offers a safer way to cross over the taller body and attack his rear. Keep the same pattern for all three hits. Do not add pursuit, acceleration, projectiles, helpers, an enrage phase or a new control.

Arm a ball flight when B throws it. A block or confirmed boss hit disarms that flight until Kittie collects and throws it again. One lingering rebound must not deal multiple hits. A resting ball cannot damage him. Keep collision inside the drawn body and tray; overhead lobs may travel over the tray to the rear, with no separate head weak point.

The solid floor and closed side walls keep misses physically recoverable. A block should produce a distinct clink and a readable rebound, without deleting, teleporting or duplicating balls. Low step geometry must not trap it in an inaccessible pocket. Preserve Kittie's three hearts, existing knockback and hurt grace. Ordinary body contact still hurts during walking and tray-down pauses. Start retries only this stage and resets the boss, gate, ball, score and lift phase.

A larger, more advanced goon

Use an approximately 24 × 32 silhouette, compared with the ordinary 16-pixel goon. A formal service coat, small bow tie, shoulder trim and a prominent vertical tray establish his rank. Keep these details inside the main sprite. Prioritize the tray over tiny decoration. The raised, lowered and crooked tray poses must differ clearly at 160 × 144. His posture and tray tell the rules without a tutorial caption.

Keep the full sprite, including tray, inside three 8-pixel columns and two 16-pixel rows. Mirror the tray with travel direction. Use three small bright uniform fasteners that dim after hits, or similarly restrained pose damage, rather than adding a sprite health bar. Boss hurt collision should fit the visible body; the tall silhouette is why the lift provides a reliable crossing route. A flat-floor vault is not a required solution.

The lift's hardware rows must stay within y64–95, and the grounded boss's within y96–127. With one ball, the intended worst cases are Kittie 5 + ball 1 + lift 4 = 10 objects on a scanline, or Kittie 5 + ball 1 + boss 3 = 9. At the highest jump, the existing compact HUD 4 + Kittie 5 + ball 1 remains 10. Count actual hardware coverage, including transparent rows, in each pose. Do not add separate tray, ornament, hit-spark or boss-meter objects that break these totals. The larger boss requirement does not authorize changing Kittie's approved sprites.

Before independent play

  • Show a frontal block, a tray-down hit and a rear hit in native frames. Confirm exactly three separate throws are needed and that a single ball can complete the fight.
  • Recover a blocked throw and an overthrown lob from both sides. Check the ball on the lift and beside the boarding step. No restart should be needed solely because the ball became inaccessible.
  • Retain consecutive native frames with the larger boss, Kittie carrying a ball, lift and HUD present. Check high jumps, underside passage, hit poses and defeat for missing sprite parts or digits.
  • Check death and Start retry, boss clear into Banquet, fifth-row selection, fresh save unlocks and schema 19538 migration. Then freeze a practice ROM for one independent complete encounter review.
