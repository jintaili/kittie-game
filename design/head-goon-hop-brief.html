Head Goon: aimed-hop revision

Approved October 4, 2026. This supersedes the patrol-and-lift encounter in the first brief.

Make Kittie earn an opening by dodging an aimed attack. Use a flat fighting floor, one recoverable wool and three successful hits. Preserve the cute cloth-goon family resemblance and the rest of the five-stage game.

Owner playtesting found that Kittie could stay in one place and hit the boss during automatic endpoint openings or rear exposure. The earlier gameplay review did not test this dominant strategy. Technical verification of that cartridge remains valid, but its gameplay acceptance is superseded.

Fight rhythm

  1. The boss approaches with his tray raised. Walking retains the ordinary goon's constant speed. The guarded pose blocks wool from either side; turning at an endpoint does not create an opening.
  2. He crouches and rattles the tray, visibly aiming a hop at Kittie's floor position. Commit to that destination before takeoff. Do not track Kittie in flight. The cue and travel time must leave a practical escape route even near the walls.
  3. He lands, fumbles and lowers the tray. This distinct recovery pose allows one successful wool hit. The ball rebounds physically and remains recoverable.
  4. At full health, one hop leads to recovery. After the first hit, two separately telegraphed hops lead to recovery. A guarded crouch between hops must look different from the tray-down opening. Each new target locks during its own wind-up.

Three hits still defeat him. Keep 100 points per successful hit, 200 for retaining the sole wool, three hearts, stage-only retry and the existing Banquet/photoshoot continuation. Do not add ammunition replacement, ball teleportation or hidden hit conditions.

Arena and presentation

Remove the playable boarding plinth and service lift from this encounter. Keep a solid flat floor, full-height wool-retaining gates and a fixed view of the whole fighting area. Tune spacing to the actual player and boss silhouettes. Room decoration may suggest a service passage, but must not imply nonexistent platforms. Preserve the approved Kittie sprite, corrected cover, four-row selector and save layout.

Use the current rounded cloth boss with ear tufts, pale eyes, small mouth, stubby feet and tray. Add only the poses needed to read wind-up, airborne commitment, guarded second wind-up and true recovery. Keep the native palette and hardware sprite limits. Difficulty comes from positioning and learning the sequence.

References

King Dedede in Kirby's Dream Land produces counterattack stars through his own attacks. Bowser in Super Mario Bros. 3 rewards baiting and dodging a committed landing. Our adaptation uses wool and a temporary tray-down opening.

Independent acceptance

Judge the complete encounter through ordinary native play. Deliberately try static throwing, corner camping, jumping in place and repeated jump-and-throw rhythms. A well-timed dodge is valid; a passive safe location must not solve the encounter. Check missed throws and recovery, both wall edges, transition between one and two hops, and whether the animations teach the opening. Preserve failed attempts as evidence and fix consequential findings within the team.
