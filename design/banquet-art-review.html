Independent art and text review · 2 October 2026 · Before implementation

Make the feast lead somewhere

The banquet already has a useful identity: warm plaster, gold bunting, dressed tables and a floral arch. Its strongest problem is the same terracotta window bay repeating every 80 pixels across all nine screens. Those large colored rectangles compete with the moving objects and make each room look interchangeable. A window even sits inside the final arch, so the destination looks like another stretch of wall.

Keep the warmth and celebration. Give the level a visible progression from a vestibule through the feast to a quiet photo recess. This should support the designer's final geometry, rather than preserve the existing obstacle arrangement for its own sake.

Part of the routeRecommended treatment
Arrival and first bellOne paired window group, then plain plaster behind the bell and wool passage. Put bunting above the room rather than across the target. By stage three, the bell and table should explain the task without instructions.
Central feastConcentrate the table settings here. Reuse restrained plate and candle tiles, with occasional gaps in the settings where wool sits or a player lands. Break the rear wall into broad bays with unequal window spacing. Keep dark horizontal lines for the actual tabletop edges.
Climb and final approachUse a narrow sideboard and a plain service-wall section to mark the change in height. Draw climbable furniture to its real collision shape. A solid sideboard should not look like an open chair; decorative furniture must not suggest a safe landing.
Final bell and photoGive the final target an uncluttered backdrop, especially if it becomes a higher shot. Let the final gate frame an open view of the floral arch. Remove the rear window from the arch and use a short, pale floor runner beneath it. The destination should be the most deliberately centered composition in the level.

Keep the furniture readable

The current raised tables read as floating slabs. A shallow cloth hem and a few place settings can establish the banquet without inventing solid-looking legs in patrol or wool paths. If supports are drawn, their treatment must clearly distinguish them from collision geometry. Keep the low wool passage visibly open and darker than the floor; do not put a decorative leg across it.

Keep the established blue water where the layout uses channels. Its familiar danger cue is more useful than inventing a new-looking pit for the final level. Let stone edging tie those openings into the hall's floor.

Remove the lesson captions

Remove all five baked captions: THE BANQUET, B UNDER A OVER, TABLE HOP, LAST BELL and PHOTO. The third level should trust the player. The room design can announce a banquet, the table clearance can reveal a low shot, and the floral arch can mark the destination. Keep the hearts and wool inventory.

The ending currently says JUST MARRIED! and KITTIE MADE IT above a box containing A TUSCAN WEDDING / A CAT WITH WOOL / THANKS FOR PLAYING. Keep KITTIE MADE IT as the single outcome line and shorten the box to THANKS FOR PLAYING. The other lines repeat information or describe the game after it is over. Preserve the existing return-to-title behavior, approved character pixels and cover. A new portrait is unnecessary.

Native implementation

Use the existing plaster, terracotta, limestone and wedding-gold palette families. Change the spacing and grouping of a small reusable tile set before adding new ornaments. Keep palette changes on 8 × 8 boundaries. Review the revised rooms at 160 × 144 with actors present, including camera positions between the nominal screen divisions. The same quiet area that makes a bell easy to see should also make its flight path legible.

Native review, 2 October 2026: arrival through central landing

The revised palette use and window spacing are a clear improvement. The arrival reads as a room, the central tables carry the visual detail, and removing the captions gives the jumps room to breathe. At native size, the pink wool, plum patrol and dark tabletop edges separate well from the outlined windows. The low table passage and its bell are legible.

One small correction would prevent a misleading cue. In central-landing frames 3471, 3481 and 3490, a tan rear post starts at y40 beside the channel. Its exposed top reads like the cap of a solid pillar, and Kittie's passing jump briefly looks like a landing on it. Extend the rear posts to the bunting or ceiling line at y24, keeping their soft background color. This makes them read as hall supports and removes the apparent landing surface.

The planned final bell at y56 is comfortably below the HUD. Keep its silhouette against plain plaster and cake decoration below roughly y72. That separation still needs confirmation in actual final-room frames.

Native review, 2 October 2026: final room and ending

The latter half keeps the same visual clarity as the opening. Final sign-off, 2 October 2026

The banquet is ready for human review from an art and text perspective. The corrected central post now reaches the bunting line and reads as a rear architectural support. It no longer presents an exposed, reachable-looking cap. This resolves the earlier finding.

The unfaded native approach confirms the wedding arch's normal appearance. Its gold curve and floral sides frame open plaster, with no competing window inside. The floor runner marks the destination quietly. The photo ending remains clear with its single outcome line and short thank-you.
