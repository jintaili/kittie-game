Independent native gameplay review · 3 October 2026

The courtyard is worth playing.
Fix the low bell's trapped wool before review.

I cleared the whole courtyard on my first attempt with two hearts remaining, three goon defeats, and two wool retained. The result screen awarded GOONS 300 + WOOL 400 = SCORE 700, then A continued into the grove. The level's difficulty was reasonable. One recovery problem undermined its new scoring incentive.

The change that matters

The low bell under the raised platform is a good idea. I defeated the goon on top, dropped back down, and threw along the floor beneath the platform to open the gate. That asks the player to change position and throwing height instead of repeating the first bell's lob.

But the wool then settled under the solid platform. At frame 1303 its center was about x91, y124, while the platform spanned roughly x72 to x136. Kittie could not walk into that space. Fifteen more frames of right input left him blocked at the side. I climbed onto the platform and stood directly above the wool at frame 1374; it remained uncollected. Down input also did not give access. I left it there and finished with two wool.

I would correct this before human review. The ordinary successful bell throw should leave a physical recovery route. Let the returning wool come clear of the platform, or give Kittie enough room to retrieve it underneath. Keep the low shot and physical recovery. A ball that stops inside collision geometry reads as a trap, and the result screen makes that trap worth 200 points. It does not feel like the consequence of a risky shot.

What worked across the whole level

The opening shelf gives a reason to try a higher route before the first bell. I found its wool without a walkthrough. The Up+B sign then explains the lob at the moment it is useful. The first bell returned its wool toward the earlier planters, and I chased it back. That short recovery trip felt fair because I could see where the ball went and could get to it.

The arch section made reward chasing a route choice. I went low to defeat its goon, then climbed back onto the shelf for the high wool. An early jump bumped the shelf harmlessly. A vertical jump beside it, followed by a move right, solved the climb. Taking both rewards cost care without threatening a long replay.

The final raised patrol was the most demanding encounter. I jumped too close, threw before landing, and took one hit. After recovering, a throw at platform height defeated it. The first damage and its inputs are preserved at frames 1165 to 1189. I understood why that approach failed, and I would keep the encounter's difficulty.

The raised patrol followed by the bell underneath made the ending distinct. It asks for two uses of the same space. The player first deals with the top, then notices something worth doing below. That is a stronger whole-level moment than another isolated gap.

Score and presentation

The clear screen explains the arithmetic well. The separate goon and wool amounts tell me what a better run would change, and BEST gives the next attempt a target. I knew the scoring premise from the review assignment, so this run cannot establish that a new player would discover wool retention before the first clear. The clear screen itself is understandable.

The water, greenery, bunting, and airborne goon defeats give the courtyard more movement around Kittie. The scene still stays sparse enough to read its platforms. Kittie's short feet are visible in the movement frames, and I did not mistake them for a separate object. I found no presentation problem that should compete with the wool recovery fix.

A native OAM inspection at the table, frame 1303, reported a peak of nine objects on a scanline, below the hardware limit of ten, with zero over-limit scanlines. Kittie's brief disappearance followed his damage reaction. I saw no unexplained sprite loss in the room. The inspection is evidence for that frame, not proof of every possible actor arrangement.
