Head Goon gameplay review

Kittie Has Other Plans · Independent native playtest · October 4, 2026 UTC

Ready for owner review. A worthwhile short boss interlude. No mandatory gameplay change from this review.

I cleared the encounter on my first blind attempt with one heart remaining. Three hits earned 300 points, and the retained balls added 200 at the exit. The second attempt was an experiment with lobs, jumps and the lift. I died after two hits. I did not read the encounter brief, builder route or walkthrough before playing.

What works

The Head Goon belongs to the regular goon family. His cloth body, little ear tufts, small mouth and stubby feet make him somewhat cute despite his size. The tray gives him a readable job and a clear guarded side. The small uniform marks survive at native resolution, though the tray carries most of the rank distinction.

The basic exchange makes sense. A shot into the raised tray bounced away without increasing the score. An unguarded hit raised the live score by 100 and tipped the tray into a reaction pose. The third hit visibly shortened his body into a defeated pose, and I could walk past him to the result screen. The physical ball stayed part of the fight: I watched it bounce and picked it up by contact.

The stage is brief, so learning the guard does not become a long punishment. On the successful attempt, I recovered from two close-contact mistakes and finished. The plinth gives a useful positional advantage. In the second attempt, jumping from it carried Kittie cleanly over the boss without losing another heart.

Difficulty and limits

Ground jumps were the awkward part for me. Two full-hold crossing attempts in the second run still cost hearts. A later jump-and-throw ended in fatal contact on the descent. Those attempts do not establish that ground crossings are impossible; they establish that the timing is tight enough that I preferred the plinth. I would keep the current difficulty for the owner's playthrough.

I did not get a sustained ride from my attempts to board the moving lift. That is an unresolved observation, not a confirmed collision defect. I passed the relevant interval, attempt 2 frames 513 to 583, to the integration owner for the separate technical verification. After play I read only the lift movement and landing code to check this observation. I made no source edits.

Evidence and exact inputs

Both attempts used the same cartridge, PyBoy 2.7.0 in color mode, a clean boot with 120 initial frames, and a paused emulator between decisions. Recordings sampled every two native frames and retained the requested step samples. Both recordings were finalized and the emulator was closed before this report was written.
