Native cover and paw animation review

Kittie follows the original photographs

The original front and side photographs remain the reference for every sprite revision. His face keeps the low oval, crown marking, closed eyes, pale nose and broad stitched grin. His low body and short legs stay. His front paw now matches the torso taupe, with one-pixel body and paw contours. The face pixels remain fixed in all 16 poses; reactions use his body and paws. One pink ball rides on his back; the beige shape under his face is his paw. He still cuddles wool while idle.

The native cover opens the game

Cover version 10 uses broad curved shading, a fuller chin, naturally asymmetric whiskers and a warm stone ledge. Press A or Start to show the stage selector over the artwork; B closes it. Sky colors cannot map onto the fur. Seven tile palettes preserve a separate UI palette. Both paws remain on the ledge. The wedding ending uses the exact gameplay sprite.

Native gameplay

These are genuine emulator frames from the final release. The earlier gameplay montage records both grove routes and the finale before the final face and menu revision. The grove climbs across stone islands; the banquet hops across tables. Patrols no longer pause or charge. Living goons move at 0.75 pixels per game update, turning at their authored endpoints.

Clearing the courtyard unlocks the grove; clearing the grove unlocks the banquet. Unlocks are saved to cartridge RAM. The title menu reads them when the game starts. Replaying an earlier stage does not remove later unlocks. There are no mid-stage checkpoints. Each stage starts with three hearts and its own six-ball supply.

Goon contact costs one heart and gives knockback plus brief protection. Losing every heart or falling into water resets the whole current stage. Wool stays where it lands until recovered; a ball lost in water stays lost until retry. Both mandatory closing courts keep a local ball available.

Clearer feedback while playing

A successful hit shakes the bell and raises its gate. The bell turns green and the raised gate keeps a green check, so the open state remains visible. The camera holds the court during the short reaction. The low tables have a shaded wool passage beneath an opaque apron. B sends wool underneath; A takes Kittie over. Hearts and wool slots now sit on dark backings, with visible outlines when empty.

Music and texture

Each stage has its own original melody and soft bass accompaniment. Courtyard and banquet themes use C major; the grove uses A minor. Throw, hit and bell cues use a separate sound channel. Stone joints, roof chips, leaf clusters, tablecloth edges, plates, windows and bunting distinguish the spaces. The title and photo scenes are quiet.
